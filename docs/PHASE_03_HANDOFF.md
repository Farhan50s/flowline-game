## Public API

```ts
createRng(seed: number): SeededRng
// rng.next(): number in [0,1)
// rng.int(minInclusive: number, maxExclusive: number): number

solveLevel(value: unknown): SolveResult
analyzeDifficulty(value: unknown): AnalysisResult
countTurns(arrow: ArrowPath): number // valid geometry expected

generateLevel(seed: number, options?: GeneratorOptions): GenerationResult
seedForLevelNumber(levelNumber: number): number
getLevel(levelNumber: number, difficulty?: Difficulty): LevelResult

ONBOARDING_LEVELS: readonly Level[]
ONBOARDING_CONCEPTS: readonly string[]
DIFFICULTY_THRESHOLDS
```

Generation success contains `level`, reverse `witness`, `analysis`, `requestedDifficulty`, `matchesRequestedDifficulty`, and deterministic proposal/candidate statistics. Failure is discriminated by `ok: false`, with `invalid-seed`, `invalid-options`, `budget-exhausted`, or `verification-failed`, plus a message and actual statistics. No invalid board is returned as a success.

`getLevel` similarly returns `ok: true` with onboarding/procedural source, Level, witness and analysis; procedural results include the complete generation result. Failure reasons are `invalid-level-number`, `unsupported-difficulty`, or `generation-failed` with the underlying generation failure. Valid difficulty overrides affect procedural levels only; fixed onboarding is unchanged by an override.

## Seed and reproducibility contract

Seeds must be integers **0..4294967295**, including zero; no coercion or truncation of unsupported values. `createRng` throws exported `SeedError` for invalid seeds. `generateLevel` instead returns a typed failure before proposing any cells. Integer sampling requires safe integer bounds, positive span, and at most 2³² possible values; invalid bounds throw without consuming the stream.

The PRNG is Mulberry32, with uint32 wrapping, explicit shifts/bitwise operations and `Math.imul`. Every generation call owns a new stream. No global RNG, clocks, UUIDs, native randomness, platform-dependent sorting, filesystem, DOM, storage, canvas, Capacitor or network APIs affect geometry. Same seed/options serialize identically, including geometry, array order, IDs, measured difficulty, witness, and proposal statistics. Timings are deliberately absent from domain results.

Stable Level IDs contain the `procedural-v1` recipe version, eight-digit hexadecimal seed and canonical normalized configuration values. Arrow IDs are insertion-order `arrow-001`, etc., unique within the Level. Version 1 is anchored by a seed-0/easy geometry JSON fixture, seed-1 PRNG reference values and fixed level-number mixing vectors. Future layout/scoring changes that affect reproducibility should introduce an explicit recipe/version change rather than silently redefining saved puzzles.

## Generator configuration and bounded construction

| Requested profile | Board | Arrow range | Minimum density |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| easy | 7×7 | 6–8 | 0.27 |
| medium | 9×9 | 11–14 | 0.36 |
| hard | 11×11 | 18–21 | 0.42 |
| expert | 13×13 | 24–27 | 0.46 |

Defaults: path length 2–7, at most 3 bends, maximum density 0.78, at most **6 candidate boards**, **64 proposals per arrow**, and **12,000 total proposals**. The total cap takes precedence over the other budgets.

Supported custom bounds: board dimensions 3–16; arrow range 2–64; path lengths 2–7; bend budget 1–3; density `0 <= min <= max <= 0.9` with positive max; candidate boards 1–16; proposals per arrow 1–256; total proposals 1–50,000. Unsupported keys, invalid values, contradictory ranges, impossible cell/density capacity and bounds that cannot support both straight/bent paths fail clearly. Standard output must have both kinds of path; a zero bend budget is intentionally unsupported.

For each insertion, choose a free head/direction with a clear forward ray, then grow the body backwards. The first backwards step is opposite the outward direction, guaranteeing the final tail-to-head segment. Growth excludes occupied/repeated cells and the head's forward ray. Reversing the collected cells produces the required tail-to-head representation. Bounds, length and bends are enforced during proposal construction.

Every eligible proposal is checked with the unchanged Phase 2 `firstBlocker` against prior arrows and `validateLevel` on the augmented board. Occupancy and old lanes are reused within a proposal batch. Candidate ranking rewards new blocking dependencies, varied directions and bends while penalizing overlong paths that consume packing space. Some slots explicitly propose straight paths, ensuring visual variety. A finite target count is sampled within the requested range; final returned counts must meet that range. The report exposes target/actual counts rather than claiming every sampled target was reached.

Complete boards must also meet minimum density, at least 20% bent paths, at least one straight path, at least two directions, initial safe fraction at most 0.85, and at least `max(1, floor(arrows/2))` blocking relationships. Final `analysis` independently verifies solvability. The reverse witness is also replayed through actual `attemptMove` and `completeEscape` before returning a frozen Level snapshot.

Matching the requested measured tier has priority. If no matching board is found within the bounded search, the closest-scoring qualified board may be returned with its **actual tier** and `matchesRequestedDifficulty: false`. If no board satisfies count/geometry/quality, the result is `budget-exhausted`; there is no arbitrary fallback board, silent count reduction outside the requested range, or unverified result.

## Solvability proof and solver

At insertion time, new arrow B has a clear exit lane relative to every older arrow. Later arrows may block B. Remove the final inserted arrow first: its lane was clear against all earlier arrows, and it has no later arrows. Repeat in reverse insertion order. Induction yields a complete legal witness. Validation also rules out own-head-ray intersections and overlapping cells, so the proof matches the actual engine geometry.

The independent solver reads **only** playable Level data and Phase 2 APIs; it does not read construction order or witness metadata. It validates, creates Zen state, selects the first currently safe ID in stable Level order, checks the deferred extraction, completes it, checks exactly one removal, and records the safe-choice trace. It performs at most the initial arrow count of successful removals.

This greedy strategy is complete: removing a safe arrow creates no occupancy and cannot block an already-safe path. If safe choices become empty with active paths remaining, no legal removal is possible. Solver results report `deadlock` and all remaining IDs, including cases where independent paths were already removed. Invalid input returns `invalid-level` issues; unexpected engine behavior is explicitly `engine-invariant`. Empty validated boards solve in zero steps. Input geometry/snapshots are never mutated.

## Difficulty model

For each arrow A, collect **every distinct** other owner along its complete head ray, not merely the nearest blocker. Multiple cells belonging to B count as one A→B relationship. Dependencies are distance ordered per arrow and stable in Level order globally.

An iterative Kahn traversal detects cycles and propagates maximum dependency depth. Depth counts **edges**: one free arrow has depth zero. Cyclic geometry is handled without recursion, with `longestChain: null`; unsolved boards have `score: null` and `tier: null` rather than a misleading expert label.

Reported metrics include arrow/occupied-cell counts, density, bent count/fraction, mean turns, direct relationships, initial safe count/fraction, longest chain/cycle flag, deterministic solver safe-choice counts and the fraction of those states with exactly one safe choice. The forced-choice metric includes the final single-arrow state and describes one deterministic solver order, not all possible human strategies.

The score is rounded to two decimals and capped at 100:

```text
visual = min(25, arrows × 0.75)
       + density × 15
       + min(16, meanTurns × 8)

blocking = min(20, relationships / arrows × 8)
         + min(24, chainEdges × 3)
         + (1 - initialSafeFraction) × 12
         + forcedChoiceFraction × 5

score = min(100, visual + blocking)
```

Empty-board terms are explicitly zero. Tier thresholds: **easy <44**, **medium 44..<60**, **hard 60..<76**, **expert ≥76**. These are stable, explainable heuristic descriptors, not a scientifically validated measure of human difficulty. Raw statistics and visual/logical score components are available for audit.

## Limitations and Phase 4 readiness

No known unresolved correctness or seed-sample failures remain. A bounded generator can still return a clear failure for custom overspecified requests or untested seeds; 1,000 passing cases are not an exhaustive survey of all 2³² seeds. The reverse proof plus production verification guarantees solvability of **returned successes**, not that every possible request can be generated within its budget.

Difficulty matching is intentionally honest rather than guaranteed: **948/1,000** sample requests match, with the easy profile the least consistent band (47 measured-medium results). Expert space was calibrated to 13×13 to reliably meet 24–27 paths, and regression seeds 760/797/855/889/951 are covered. Boards were algorithmically tested, not human playtested or difficulty-rated on devices. Future calibration must retain or version the deterministic recipe; density alone should not determine labels.

Phase 4 can consume `getLevel`/`generateLevel` results, display the measured tier, and connect gameplay rendering/input to the unchanged state engine. Keep arrows occupied during `animating`, render bent extraction along the actual tip/body route, and commit only through `completeEscape`. Cancel stale animation callbacks on restart/level switch and gate attempts on `ready`. Do not rigidly translate bent paths through occupied cells when rules check only the head lane. Treat Level snapshots/collections as read-only.

Generation can take tens of milliseconds on this desktop; schedule or prefetch between levels rather than during a gesture/animation, and measure actual Android performance when native prerequisites are available. Android compilation remains blocked by Java 21 / Android Studio / SDK setup from the preceding handoff. Synchronization is not an APK build.
